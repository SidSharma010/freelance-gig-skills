# Pricing benchmarks — reference

**Honesty note first:** the figures below are rough orientation bands based
on general, widely-observed freelance market patterns — they are not pulled
from live platform data, and they will drift out of date. Treat them as a
sanity-check starting point, not a quoted market rate. `pricing-advisor`
should state this caveat whenever it uses this file, and should weight the
user's own stated experience and market over these bands whenever the two
disagree.

## Why rates vary this much

The same skill (say, WordPress development) can reasonably command a 5-10x
spread depending on: client's home market (US/EU/AU clients generally pay
more than price-sensitive markets), platform (direct clients off-platform
typically pay more than marketplace bids), freelancer's portfolio depth and
reviews, and how commoditized the specific task is (a well-templated task
like "install a WordPress theme" prices far lower than a custom integration).

## Rough experience-tier bands (USD/hour equivalent, illustrative only)

| Tier | Rough band | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| New to the platform, building reviews | Lower end of what covers your time fairly | Underpricing here is common and often necessary short-term — but say so explicitly, don't let it become the anchor |
| Established, solid portfolio, repeat clients | Meaningfully above the entry band | Reviews + a visible portfolio are what justify this jump, not just time elapsed |
| Specialist / hard-to-find skill combination | Can be several multiples of the general band | Rare combinations (e.g. a specific framework + a regulated industry's compliance knowledge) price on scarcity, not just effort |

## Fixed-price vs hourly

- Fixed-price rewards efficient work but only if the scope is genuinely
  fixed — flag scope ambiguity before agreeing to a flat price.
- Hourly protects against scope creep but caps upside — some clients avoid
  it outright due to past bad experiences; read the job post's tone.

## What actually moves a rate up over time

- A visible, specific portfolio (named outcomes, not vague descriptions)
- Reviews that mention results, not just "great to work with"
- Repeat clients — a rate increase is easier to ask an existing happy
  client for than to win from a new one
- Specialization — a narrow, credible niche usually out-earns a broad
  generalist profile at the same experience level

## How pricing-advisor should use this file

Use it to sanity-check a number the user proposes, or to give a *shape* of
reasoning (tier, spread, what would justify the higher end) — never to
state a specific dollar figure as if it were verified current market data.
If the user wants real current numbers for their specific niche, say so and
suggest they check recent job posts in that category on the platform
directly, since that's more current than anything static in this file.
