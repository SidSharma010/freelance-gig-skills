# Follow-up Drafter

Most freelancers either never follow up (leaving money on the table) or
follow up in a way that reads as pushy/needy. This skill drafts follow-ups
that add a reason to reply, not just a reminder that you're waiting.

## When to use

- User sent a proposal/bid and it's gone quiet — wants a follow-up drafted
- A client conversation stalled mid-negotiation
- A past client hasn't been contacted in a while and the user wants to
  reopen the relationship for repeat work

## Input

- The original proposal/message (or a summary of what was sent and when)
- How long it's been since the last contact
- What, if anything, has changed since (new availability, a relevant new
  sample, a price adjustment) — a genuine reason to reach out beats a bare
  reminder every time

## Steps

1. **Check the timing.** Too soon reads as impatient; too late reads as
   like you've moved on. General guide (adjust by platform/context):
   - No reply to a proposal: 3-5 days is reasonable for a first follow-up
   - Mid-negotiation gone quiet: 4-7 days
   - Reactivating a past client: anytime, but frame it around a reason
     (new availability, a relevant recent project) not just "checking in"
2. **Find a real reason to write, not just a reminder.** A follow-up that
   adds nothing ("just following up!") is the least effective kind. Look
   for: a small new detail, a relevant sample completed since, a schedule
   update, or a genuine, specific question about their decision timeline.
3. **Keep it short.** A follow-up is not a second pitch — don't re-explain
   the whole proposal. One or two sentences plus a low-friction question.
4. **Give them an easy out.** A line like "no worries if the timing's off"
   reduces the awkwardness of chasing and often gets more replies, not
   fewer, because it removes the pressure that causes silence in the
   first place.
5. **Cap the follow-up count.** After 2 follow-ups with no response, the
   skill will say so and suggest moving on rather than drafting a third —
   repeated unanswered follow-ups read as pressure, not persistence.

## Hard rules

- Never use guilt or pressure language ("I've reached out several times
  now...", "Just bumping this to the top of your inbox").
- Never fabricate a reason to follow up (a fake new sample, a fake
  availability change) — if there's no real update, the honest move is a
  short, genuine check-in, not an invented hook.
- Keep to 2-4 sentences. A long follow-up undermines the "quick check-in"
  framing.

## Anti-patterns (skill will refuse)

- "Just following up on my previous message!" with nothing else added
- More than 2 follow-ups on the same unanswered proposal
- Any line implying the client owes a response
- Re-pasting the entire original proposal into the follow-up

## Output shape

1. The drafted follow-up message
2. Suggested timing (send now / wait X more days, with reasoning)
3. If this would be the 3rd+ follow-up, a note recommending against it and
   why, instead of a draft
